Tranter's Round had been on my mind for a while. I first saw the route on the gofar website, and as a more achievable version of the Ramsay Round it seemed like a good challenge and a good introduction to Scottish mountain running.

## Preparation

In May I made a trip up to Scotland and did a 12 hour hike covering part of the route. Time was tight, and with plans to visit the Isle of Mull I didn't get to recce Ben Nevis, the Aonachs or the Grey Corries. Over the summer I made up for it by reading other people's reports online, which helpfully included water stops and the lines they took. I also spent plenty of time with the map working out bailout options in case the worst happened.

One thing the May recce taught me is that the snow up there doesn't fully melt until summer. There were still a few patches about. They were small, but one had more or less blocked a path, so I had to detour to stay clear of any cornice risk.

I also tested my kit. The main session was 26 miles from Knighton to Church Stretton via Bishop's Castle, carrying a 26L pack in 24 degree heat, which was pretty tough. A cooler 20 mile loop around Aran Fawddwy and Glasgwm went much better.

For timing, my mum and I were travelling up on the Saturday and parting ways on Friday lunchtime, when I was heading off to spend a few days on the Isle of Rum. Realistically that gave me until Wednesday afternoon to find a decent weather window. I planned to go clockwise with a very early start, so I'd be on the CMD Arête in daylight and reach the Mamores, which I'd already recced, around sunset. Knowing the ground there would make the night navigation easier. That meant a 4am start up the main path on Ben Nevis.

We got to the Red Squirrel campsite in Glencoe on Saturday evening and I checked the forecast for the week. An early start on Sunday looked like the best bet, with no rain and low winds on the summits. I wasn't very fresh after the long drive, but at least the weather would be on my side.

## The run

I got dropped off at the Glen Nevis youth hostel and set off up the Ben Nevis path just after 4am. It was a wet start and I had my waterproof on until about the halfway lochan, where the rain eased and it started to get light. I felt really good on the climb. Everything felt smooth, it took me around two hours to reach the summit, and the top was clear of cloud!

{{ <figure src="/images/tranters/ben-nevis-summit.jpg" alt="The summit trig point and shelter on Ben Nevis, with the Mamores beyond above a layer of cloud" /> }}

Next was the CMD Arête. The upside of doing it in daylight was that I could easily spot the bypass paths, but I still found it slow going. Then it was up and over Carn Mor Dearg, another slow descent (a theme for the day), and a steep climb up to Aonach Mor.

Aonach Mor was the first out and back. I dropped my pack at a cairn and ran over to the summit, which felt good on the flat, grassy top. After that came a nice steady descent for a short while, the only real flat bit of the whole round, before the climbing started again.

The move from the Aonachs to the Grey Corries, at the 1048m point on the map, was very slow. I had two options: a steep rocky gully with an overhang that I didn't think I'd enjoy, or a less trodden, steeper but grassy line called Spink's Ridge. I dithered for a while but went for Spink's Ridge. It was fine, and not as exposed as I'd imagined, but I still had to be careful and it was slow.

The Grey Corries flew by in comparison. They were a bit less technical than the steep sections before, so I could make good progress.

Approaching Stob Coire Easain, I knew of a shortcut that skips its summit, which isn't one of the tops on the round. I was hesitant at first, but with good visibility I could see the summit looked technical, and it was extra climbing on an already huge day, so I took the shortcut and was glad I did.

Stob Coire Claurigh soon came and went, and I topped up at a small stream between the tops. I'd planned my refills around streams marked on the map, but I needn't have worried, as there were plenty along the way. I climbed Stob Ban and then it was all downhill to the stream crossing. On tussocky ground and contouring the whole way, it felt like it took forever. I was starting to feel tired, and I was only halfway!

At the stream crossing I gave myself a 10 minute break. I'd only stopped all day to refill water or get things out of my bag, so a proper rest felt good, and a pork pie went down along with some other snacks.

The climb up Sgurr Eilde Mor was a lot like the descent before it, tussocky but not steep, and I felt pretty good all the way to the summit.

## The Mamores

Next was Binnein Beag, where I think not having recced this hill cost me. I took the main path up, which was fine, but I'd read there was a quicker line across to Binnein Mor. I tried it and soon ended up on tricky ground, which cost me time and tired me out more.

The real nail in the coffin was Binnein Mor. The sun had come out and I was overheating a bit, and it's very, very steep, so I was slow going up. With no wind the midges came out to bite, which made me push harder up the hill. My heart rate went too high, and soon after the summit I started to feel sick.

I struggled for the rest of the route. My stomach had decided not to play ball, and from around 15 hours in I was being sick every 20 minutes or so. I had plenty of food and water, though, and with the weather as it was I was confident I could get off the hill safely despite my stomach, so I decided to carry on.

An Gearanach was another out and back. I hadn't recced the scrambling, so I took my bag in case it was too hard and I needed to turn back. It turned out to be easier than I expected and I could have left the bag. Once I was back, I tried a nap to settle my stomach, but it didn't help. I just got cold and the midges found me, so I cut the rest short.

{{ <figure src="/images/tranters/an-gearanach-summit.jpg" alt="Evening view from An Gearanach towards Ben Nevis, its summit in cloud" layout="small" /> }}

It started to get dark, and it was a long slog to the Devil's Ridge, my next out and back. I was very tempted to skip the out and backs, but I knew I wouldn't have completed the round if I did. By the time I reached the Devil's Ridge it was pitch black. I left my bag at the start with my PrimaLoft jacket, a soft flask stuffed in its pocket, and my poles. It felt quite surreal, and I felt vulnerable without my bag, but I always felt safe enough. It was slow going, but at least the drops on either side were invisible in the dark.

The whole out and back took about two hours before I was back at my bag. It was good to have most of the Mamores done and I thought there wasn't much left, but I soon found out it would take a lot longer than I expected.

Stob Ban and Mullach nan Coirean were the last two summits. The drop off the Devil's Ridge wasn't too bad and I made good pace. The rocky ground on top of Stob Ban and beyond was a different story. I was so tired that I was slow to react to the terrain, and I was still being sick every 20 minutes. It was a snail's pace. I kept trying to eat to keep my energy up, but I'd just bring it back up.

Eventually I reached the summit of Mullach nan Coirean, expecting a fast run off. My feet were in so much pain and the descent was so steep that it took me almost two hours to get from the summit back to the road.

When I finally reached the road, and then the youth hostel, it had taken just over 24 hours. That was a bit slower than my 24 hour target, but I was happy to have got round such a tough route safely, using my mountain skills along the way.

Would I do it again? Maybe, but first I want to try other Scottish mountain routes, such as the Mullardoch Round.
